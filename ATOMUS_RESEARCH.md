# Atomus / Triple A: public implementation evidence

Researched 2026-10-26. Primary sources only. No APK downloading, decompilation, or developer contact. This note records public evidence; it does not establish that the current approximation matches Atomus.

## What is publicly available

**SungLab publishes its C++/OpenGL engine, but the inspected public material does not contain a complete Atomus application or its effect equations.** [StarEngine](https://github.com/sunglab/StarEngine) is MIT licensed, copyright Sung Woo Choi 2010–2017. The developer's [2016 engine announcement](https://github.com/sunglab/sunglab.github.io/blob/3abec4c157886a9bc9e9ff12c09de4a58b2757f0/_posts/2016-12-29-StarEngine-post10.html) independently connects that repository to SungLab. Source observations below are pinned to engine commit `46ef40a9051efd95265386282a06cbfb6ee39186`.

- [Current Atomus iOS listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/atomus/id1605327908): up to 12,000 particles, ten simultaneous touches, separate particle size/count/trail-length controls, three color modes. This is a product claim, not measured performance on our WebView.
- [Atomus Android listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sunglab.atomus): mathematical/physical graphics and historical NEON optimization; release notes mention a compute-to-vertex-shader optimization. - [Atomus 3D listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sunglab.geoart): 10,000 particles and similar controls. This is a distinct app; do not confuse its count with 2D Atomus.

## Practical lessons for Still — proposed implementation, not recovered Atomus code

1. Use live contact geometry as simulation input. Preserve per-contact identity and previous positions. Angular ordering, centroid, and closed perimeter have direct developer-source precedent. They provide a stronger starting point than insertion order or fixed ellipse presets, while requiring visual validation of the actual steering behavior.
2. Keep particles persistent, with current position, previous endpoint, velocity, and stable variation between particles. Changing fingers should bend the existing population continuously. The exact attraction, orbital force, checkpoint selection, damping, and release behavior remain reconstruction choices.
3. Treat motion, filament width, persistence, and exposure as separate controls. For our stated visual target, many fine, colored trajectories should form broad ribbons through density. Do not paint broad opaque white ribbons as a substitute. Additive light accumulation and a decaying history buffer are plausible rendering experiments; neither is confirmed as Atomus's algorithm.
4. Preserve dark loop interiors by limiting blur and accumulation, keeping individual filament energy low, and reserving white for actual dense overlap. This follows the user's reference target, not a published developer equation.
5. Implement the smallest useful experiment in the existing WebGL1 renderer, then compare identical one-, two-, three-, and five-contact sequences against a playable reference. Still contacts, slow deformation, crossed contacts, release, and cancellation should all be inspected. Particle count and 60 FPS marketing claims are not visual acceptance criteria.

If adapting any MIT source verbatim, preserve its copyright and license notice. Reimplement algorithms carefully: the old particle header uses `reserve()` followed by indexed writes without `resize()`, so it should not be copied mechanically as production C++.

## Focused source recheck — 2026-10-27

The pinned [complete engine tree](https://github.com/sunglab/StarEngine/tree/46ef40a9051efd95265386282a06cbfb6ee39186) and likely effect/view entry points were rechecked before the next Stardust rewrite. This found an additional **active**, rather than commented-out, head/tail primitive in [`StarView.h`](https://github.com/sunglab/StarEngine/blob/46ef40a9051efd95265386282a06cbfb6ee39186/ui/StarView.h#L155): `sync()` in `SETVIEW::LINES` first copies each previous head into its paired tail, then copies `rect_center[i]` into the head. `setView(LINES)` allocates two positions, colors, and indices per particle using `resize()`. This is a usable source-grounded representation to adapt independently of the incomplete older `StarParticleManager`.

The same view still leaves `update()` and `render()` pure virtual. [`StarScene`](https://github.com/sunglab/StarEngine/blob/46ef40a9051efd95265386282a06cbfb6ee39186/ui/starscene.h) only dispatches those methods and touch callbacks to child views. [`StarEffect.h`](https://github.com/sunglab/StarEngine/blob/46ef40a9051efd95265386282a06cbfb6ee39186/effect/StarEffect.h) includes Fluid and Perlin; it does not supply an Atomus effect. The inspected concrete [`StarBG`](https://github.com/sunglab/StarEngine/blob/46ef40a9051efd95265386282a06cbfb6ee39186/ui/StarBG.h) and [`StarButton`](https://github.com/sunglab/StarEngine/blob/46ef40a9051efd95265386282a06cbfb6ee39186/ui/StarButton.h) updates animate UI geometry, not touch-driven particle currents. No additional working Atomus steering law or its caller was recovered from this focused pass.

`StarView.h` also contains an additive `glBlendFunc(GL_SRC_ALPHA, GL_ONE)` **commented render template** near its end. It provides developer-source precedent for additive rendering in the engine, but does not establish Atomus's actual blend state or trail lifetime. A longer history of curved filaments, energy-preserving touch steering, release forces, and any bloom remain our implementation choices and must be validated against the supplied references rather than described as recovered Atomus code.

## v0.6 implementation boundary

The new JavaScript solver retains particle positions and velocities across hold, drag, release and settling. It uses the public contact centroid/angular ordering/perimeter and paired endpoint update, with original covariance-driven spring fields and checkpoint steering. Compact or nearly collinear touch groups retain a nondegenerate orbital field; this avoids a stationary dot when checkpoints coincide. Independent energy bands preserve a broad moving population during sustained holds. Screen boundaries are consistent before and after release, and release adds momentum instead of replacing it on a timer.

The renderer extends the public paired-line primitive into short connected history segments. Bright additive filament cores and small Gaussian halos come directly from the individual particles. Connected endpoints are reused in 16-bit indexed batches; no full-screen history or blur buffer is accumulated. A coarse color-separated velocity grid diffuses coherent free currents without prescribing paths, while sparse currents gain light gradually as they spread. This is an original reconstruction, not a recovered Atomus shader or complete C++ engine runtime port. Captured gestures and regression measurements document our implementation; physical Android frame rate and subjective matching to Atomus remain device checks.

## Visual comparison from inspected screenshots — 2026-10-27

Two actual screenshots linked by the [official Android listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sunglab.atomus) were downloaded for local analysis and visually inspected: the [crossing atom](https://play-lh.googleusercontent.com/O5V1UvCcb6lW4f4VyZF_QR11P2ePesN58RXzfFZ5QoNT_25WpFTchrj53uZvPyiMJb7rivNVI1MIT2oMGwpl_YY=w526-h296) and [finger-shaped elliptical ribbon](https://play-lh.googleusercontent.com/uKDmvpP4mkabDiCyvF6CexpRUs1rBGyBplU23PA_k7ANO1V8Z5JyNeImXbM2UEtRzFMAM3zA7bKxoi8YnUsfV-E=w526-h296). The user's matching atom image and dense multi-finger reference were also inspected. These proprietary reference images are not bundled or reused in the app; local copies and provenance are outside Git in `work/atomus-reference-v6`.

Visible reference characteristics: dense, fine, continuous luminous threads form the broad bands; bright ivory/cyan cores coexist with saturated blue/red/gold fringes. Crossings can become very bright, while loop interiors stay dark. A few fainter filaments overshoot the main bands. The elliptical ribbon has variable band width and local finger-shaped compression, rather than a uniformly stroked outline. These are image observations, not recovered rendering equations or claims about video motion.

Compared with the early v0.6 captures `stardust-held-20s.png`, `stardust-three.png`, and `stardust-folded.png`, our geometry and black interiors are closer, but those early ribbons were mostly saturated low-luminance color, with small white crossing patches and scratchy/dashed outer threads. The highest-value refinement is brighter **sharp particle filament cores**, with saturated fringes and restrained local glow, rather than more full-screen blur or a painted white marker. Preserve a dense main current plus a smaller population of faint stray filaments. The early `stardust-release-2.4s.png` was a fairly uniform dim web across the whole screen; it loses the strong hierarchy visible in the held reference compositions. The supplied official stills do not establish Atomus's release behavior, so release changes must be evaluated as our own interaction design.

The final pass responds with a dense central population and fainter broad energy fringes, higher sharp-filament exposure, small Gaussian particle halos, and gradual sparse-current emission. Local color-separated velocity diffusion preserves coherent free currents; no release timer replaces velocities. These changes were recaptured and checked in the running browser. The complete Atomus simulation is still not claimed.
